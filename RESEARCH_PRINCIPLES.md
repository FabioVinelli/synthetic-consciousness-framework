# Research principles

SCF investigates computational mechanisms through explicit hypotheses and observable outcomes. Its philosophical influences do not substitute for experimental evidence.

## Standards

1. **Falsifiability.** State what outcome would count against each empirical hypothesis. Claims that cannot be tested must be identified as philosophical, metaphorical, or speculative rather than treated as experimental conclusions.
2. **Measurable hypotheses.** Define the mechanism, intervention, comparison, task, metric, resource budget, and decision criterion before interpreting results.
3. **Reproducibility.** Record model and provider versions, prompts, configurations, input provenance, evaluation procedures, resource use, and failure conditions sufficiently for permitted replication.
4. **Epistemic humility.** Preserve uncertainty, disagreements, limitations, and unresolved questions. Do not equate persuasive language or agent self-report with verified understanding or subjective experience.
5. **Observation versus inference.** Report what was directly recorded separately from the explanation proposed for it. Every inference must remain bounded by the conditions and evidence supporting it.
6. **Model/provider neutrality.** Define mechanisms independently of vendor-specific APIs or model identities. Provider integrations are replaceable; claims of generality require testing beyond a single configuration.
7. **Domain neutrality.** Keep the core model-, project-, work-, and industry-agnostic. Application-specific rules belong in later adapters and evaluation cases.
8. **Negative-results reporting.** Report null, adverse, and inconclusive outcomes as well as favorable ones. Include failures, invalid outputs, overhead, and exclusions with their reasons.
9. **Ablation testing.** Remove or vary individual mechanisms to assess their contribution. Include strong baselines and resource-matched comparisons so that additional computation is not mistaken for a mechanism-specific gain.
10. **Clear intellectual attribution.** Preserve the distinction between original source material, later summaries, computational adaptations, and new findings. Disclose AI assistance and do not treat AI-generated prose as independent source verification.
11. **No inference of consciousness from utility.** Better task performance or richer self-modeling does not establish sentience or phenomenal consciousness.
12. **Deliberate publication.** Keep private source conversations and proprietary-rights paperwork outside incidental publication. Licensing decisions are separate from research attribution.

## Epistemic categories

Each substantive claim should identify its category and evidence status. Several categories may inform a discussion, but their boundaries must remain explicit.

| Category | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Established evidence | Identify supporting sources, their scope, and material limitations; the label remains revisable |
| Active research | Identify the unresolved question and competing accounts; ongoing investigation is not a settled result |
| Hypothesis | State a testable prediction, comparison, and possible falsifying outcome |
| Philosophy | Attribute the conceptual or metaphysical position without presenting it as experimentally established |
| Conscientiology-derived concepts | Identify the intellectual tradition and source; scientific standing is not implied by inclusion |
| Architectural metaphor | Name the software analogy and specify where it stops being a claim about the source concept |
| Speculation | Mark unsupported possibilities explicitly and do not report them as findings |
